What it catches

Clepeus checks each page and webmail message you open. A warning card appears for strong signs; weaker ones are listed when you click the shield in your toolbar.

Hidden instructions for AI assistants
Some pages carry text that people can't see but AI tools can read: text that isn't displayed, text the same color as its background, comments in the page code, and invisible characters. If you ask an AI assistant to summarize or act on that page, the hidden text can steer it. Clepeus shows you the text so you can decide.
Signs of a scam
Pressure to act fast, requests for gift cards or crypto, requests for passwords or codes, surprise prizes, demands for secrecy, and threats. One of these alone is ordinary. Several together trigger a warning.
Links that lie
A link whose text shows one website but leads to another, a link that goes to a raw number address, and addresses built from look-alike characters.

Your pages stay on your computer

  • Every check runs inside your browser, on your own machine.
  • The extension makes no network requests. Nothing you read is sent anywhere.
  • It stores two settings: whether checking is on, and the sites you chose to trust.
  • No accounts, no analytics, no advertising.

What it can't do

Clepeus works from a fixed set of rules. It will miss some scams and some hidden instructions, and it will sometimes flag a page that is fine. A page with no warning is not guaranteed to be safe.

You can mark a site as trusted, or turn checking off, from the toolbar popup.
